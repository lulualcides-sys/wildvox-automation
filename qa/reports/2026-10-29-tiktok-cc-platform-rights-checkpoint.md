## Official source checks

- Creative Commons FAQ: https://creativecommons.org/faq/index.html — section “Do I need to worry about website terms of service when I share CC-licensed content on social media platforms?” CC licenses do **not** authorize the reuser to sublicense third-party CC material. The FAQ also explains that social-media sharing is not inherently forbidden, because the platform may need only rights to post rather than a grant of copyright in the third-party component. Attribution and all applicable license conditions remain necessary.
- TikTok US Terms of Service (updated July 15, 2026): https://in.tiktok.com/legal/page/us/terms-of-service/en — section 3.5 requests broad assignable, sublicensable rights to user content, and a representation that the uploader has all rights needed to submit and distribute it. These are **US terms**; the applicable account jurisdiction and Metricool posting pathway have not been independently confirmed.
- Source-by-source Commons authors and license records: qa/credits/2026-10-29.md and qa/commons-license-audit-2026-10-29.json. Forty creator/license matches from the existing Commons audit are evidence of provenance, **not** a platform sublicense or permission from all creators.

## Unresolved release gates

1. Verify the applicable TikTok/Metricool terms and whether the uploader can grant only rights in the original WildVox portions while third-party footage remains directly licensed under its CC terms; do not assume platform sublicensing of Commons footage.
2. Verify ShareAlike obligations for each adapted clip (license version, adaptation notice, access to license text and original source, and whether the adapted footage itself is offered under the correct license).
3. Verify the full credit notice survives the actual scheduled TikTok post, including the creator, license link, original work link, and modifications. A text-only caption and a GitHub credits page do not prove final platform display.
4. Where compliance is uncertain, prefer verified CC0/public-domain footage or obtain direct creator permission explicitly covering the distribution platform and adaptations. Do not automatically relabel CC BY-SA content as CC BY or proprietary.
5. Editorial and motion QA must also pass for the **new** render; technical CI alone is insufficient.
